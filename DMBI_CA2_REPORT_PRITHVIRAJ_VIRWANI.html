3. Data Preprocessing & Feature Engineering Pipeline

Raw HR data requires rigorous cleaning and transformation before applying Data Mining classifiers. The following 5-stage preprocessing workflow was implemented in Python (pandas & scikit-learn):

1. Quality Audit0 Nulls & 0 Duplicates Verified
2. Feature PruningDropped 4 Constant / ID Columns
3. EncodingBinary Map + One-Hot (44 Features)
4. ScalingStandardScaler (Z-Score)
5. BalancingStratified 80/20 + Class Weights
  1. Missing Value & Duplicate Check: Confirmed df.isnull().sum().sum() == 0 and df.duplicated().sum() == 0 across all 1,470 rows.
  2. Zero-Variance & Identifier Pruning: Removed 4 non-informative features: EmployeeCount (always 1), Over18 (always 'Y'), StandardHours (always 80), and EmployeeNumber (arbitrary ID), reducing dimensionality from 35 to 31 columns.
  3. Categorical Encoding: Mapped binary attributes (Attrition, OverTime, Gender) to 0/1. Applied One-Hot Encoding (drop_first=True) to multi-class nominal columns (Department, BusinessTravel, EducationField, JobRole, MaritalStatus), yielding 44 clean numeric predictors.
  4. Feature Scaling: Standardized continuous attributes (MonthlyIncome, Age, DistanceFromHome) using StandardScaler so linear models are not biased by scale differences.
  5. Class Imbalance Handling & Stratified Split: Because only 16.12% of employees attrited, we applied a Stratified 80/20 Train-Test Split (1,176 train / 294 test) and configured class_weight="balanced" to prevent models from trivially predicting "No Attrition".

5. Data Mining Algorithms Implemented

To rigorously satisfy and exceed the CA2 requirement of implementing at least one Data Mining algorithm, we implemented and benchmarked three supervised classification algorithms on the exact same stratified test set (294 employees: 247 Stayed, 47 Left):

  1. Decision Tree Classifier (CART – Gini Impurity): Partitions employees recursively by maximizing information gain (minimizing Gini impurity). Produces transparent, human-readable IF–THEN business rules directly usable by HR managers.
  2. Random Forest Classifier (Bagging Ensemble – 200 Trees): Builds 200 decorrelated decision trees using bootstrap aggregating and random feature subsets, reducing overfitting and delivering superior overall accuracy and feature importance rankings.
  3. Logistic Regression (Linear Probabilistic Baseline): Computes log-odds probabilities for employee attrition after Z-score scaling, providing the highest Recall (74.47%) for catching potential leavers.

7. Interpretation of Results & Key Insights Obtained

1. OverTime is the #1 Burnout Catalyst (30.53% vs 10.44%): Employees consistently logging overtime are nearly 3x more likely to resign. Combined with low job satisfaction, overtime triggers immediate resignation risk.
2. Compensation Gap Drives Churn ($4,787 vs $6,833): The mean monthly salary of departing employees ($4,787) is 29.9% lower than retained staff ($6,833), proving that entry-level and under-market pay bands suffer severe leakage.
3. Equity Vesting Anchors Retention: Employees with zero stock options (StockOptionLevel = 0) exhibit a 24.4% attrition rate, whereas staff granted Level 1 or Level 2 equity drop to just 7.6%–9.4% turnover.
4. Early-Tenure Cliff (0–2 Years): Nearly 43% of all resignations occur within an employee's first 2 years at the company, highlighting a critical onboarding and early career-growth gap.
5. High-Churn Pockets in Sales & Lab Technicians: The Sales department experiences the highest attrition (20.63%), particularly among Sales Representatives (39.8% role turnover) who travel frequently and work overtime.
